// PROJECT ARCHITECTURE: THE 3 IN-DEPTH DESIGN PILLARS

This fighter design project required a rigorous systems engineering approach. The aircraft was fully sized across three major inseparable pillars: external aerodynamics & lifting surfaces, internal volume & service layout, and strict management of mass and center of gravity to secure the flight envelope.

1. Aerodynamics & Delta-Canard Wing

Role: Transonic flow control and dogfighting agility

Architecture Choice: The aircraft relies on a delta wing with a surface area of 27.24 m², characterized by a leading-edge sweep angle of 41° and a relative profile thickness set at 4%. This geometry helps push back compressibility effects and significantly reduces wave drag during transonic acceleration.

Aerodynamic Coupling: The addition of active forward canard planes generates controlled leading-edge vortices that energize the upper surface of the main wing at high angles of attack. This gives the aircraft excellent instantaneous maneuverability while ensuring a safe approach speed of 125 knots upon landing.

Design Office Justification: Subsonic and supersonic profile studies under Excel validated maximum lift and mapped aerodynamic fineness across the various flight regimes required by specifications (low-altitude interception and tactical patrol).

2. Internal Layout & Fuel Tanks

Role: Three-dimensional integration of services and fuel payload

Volumetric Optimization: Internal component integration underwent comprehensive 3D modeling using 3DEXPERIENCE. The goal was to house two turbojets while preserving sealed bays capable of holding 3,836 kg of internal fuel.

Circuits and Services: The routing of air intake ducts was optimized to match high-speed flow kinematics. Additionally, main landing gear bays were compacted against the sides of the central fuselage to prevent any interference with wing boxes.

Payload Compromise: This layout optimizes available space for mission equipment (radar system, weapons bays, and defensive avionics) while complying with geometric clearance constraints imposed by the structure.

3. Mass & Center of Gravity Management

Role: Guaranteeing airworthiness and flight stability

Mass Breakdown: Rigorous establishment of the mass estimate evaluated the Operating Empty Mass (OEM) at 8,535 kg, with a Maximum Takeoff Weight (MTOW) reaching 20,413 kg in armed and refueled mission configuration.

Longitudinal Stability: Analysis of center of gravity curves ensures that the aircraft's Static Margin remains framed between 2.63% and 9.85% across the entire flight envelope, ensuring sound and balanced behavior during subsonic/supersonic transitions.

Load Factor: Structural strength calculation notes also validated limit and ultimate load tolerances for an operational load factor of 4g, ensuring cell safety during tight maneuvers.
