// METHODOLOGICAL ARCHITECTURE & 3D DESIGN

The engineering approach relied on a rigorous workflow ranging from parametric surface modeling under SolidWorks 2023 to finite volume numerical simulations via SolidWorks Flow Simulation.

1. FIA 2024 Regulatory Framework & Geometric Modeling

Strict compliance with aerodynamic templates and integration of moving mechanisms

The wing architecture was entirely modeled in compliance with the limits imposed by the Formula 1 technical regulations (2024 season). Overall dimensions include a maximum width of 1230 mm and a total height of 600 mm.

The assembly integrates the main plane, the endplate, the upper movable flap, and the Drag Reduction System (DRS) actuator allowing a calibrated opening of 8.5 cm as well as a maximum flap angle of 20° in high-downforce configuration.

Figure 2: Mesh of the complete wing assembly.

2. Physical Modeling & Fundamental CFD Equations

Mathematical formalization of flow, lift, and drag

To characterize aerodynamic behavior, lift and drag forces were evaluated using fundamental fluid dynamics relations based on dynamic pressure, air density, flow velocity, as well as dimensionless coefficients Cl and Cd.

The numerical solver implements Reynolds-Averaged Navier-Stokes (RANS) equations associated with two-equation turbulence models (k-epsilon and k-omega). These models capture velocity gradients, boundary layer separations along airfoil profiles, and tip vortex dissipation at the endplates with high fidelity.

3. Boundary Conditions & Operational Scenarios (Monaco vs. Mexico)

Comparative analysis against drastic variations in air density

The comparative study focused on two Grand Prix environments with radically opposite atmospheric characteristics:

  • Monaco Grand Prix (Low speed, maximum downforce): Flow velocity set at 289.9 km/h (80.53 m/s), ambient temperature of 20.05°C, atmospheric pressure of 1005 hPa, and standard air density of 1.225 kg/m^3.
  • Mexico Grand Prix (High speed, critical altitude): Simulated top speed at 362.5 km/h (99 m/s). Due to the high altitude of the circuit (approx. 2300 meters), air density drops significantly to 0.977 kg/m^3, directly impacting downforce efficiency.

4. Numerical Results & Comparative Force Analysis

Quantitative evaluation of aerodynamic performance gains

Analysis of results from SolidWorks Flow Simulation highlights the effectiveness of DRS open versus closed configurations:

  • Monaco Configuration: Without DRS, the wing generates 1656 N of downforce for 796 N of drag. DRS activation reduces downforce to 1298 N and decreases drag to 514 N, lowering residual turbulence intensity at 10 meters to 3.69%.
  • Mexico Configuration: Under the combined effect of increased speed (362.5 km/h) and low air density (0.977 kg/m^3), opening the DRS drops downforce from 2154 N to 1590 N and drag from 1018 N to 670 N, demonstrating crucial optimization of top speed on straights despite atmospheric thinning.

// TECHNICAL ASSESSMENT & FUTURE OUTLOOK

This applied research project consolidated mastery of advanced numerical simulation tools and validated the critical impact of the DRS system on the lift-to-drag trade-off of Formula 1 single-seaters.
