// MISSION ARCHITECTURE & KINEMATIC MODELING

The problem revolves around a pursuing drone initially positioned at [0, 0, 50] with an initial velocity of [10, 0, 0] m/s. The three ground targets move at a constant identical velocity equal to [1.5, 1.5, 0] m/s, while a fixed observer is stationed at [-200, -20, 100].

1. Study No. 1: Kinematic Guidance & Rendezvous Phase

Use of a virtual target to satisfy the constant altitude constraint

To prevent any unwanted altitude variation toward targets located on the ground (z=0), a virtual target is projected onto the drone's flight plane at z = 50 m. The attraction vector is defined by the relative position between the drone and this virtual target.

A sequencing logic integrated via a sequential switching block (Multiport Switch) allows the drone to switch instantly from one objective to the next as soon as the horizontal distance crosses the critical threshold of 10 meters.

2. Guidance by Alignment on the Observer–Virtual Target Line

Line-of-sight control and application of a damped second-order system

To break away from the disordered trajectories of simple attraction guidance, orthogonal alignment guidance is implemented. The dynamics of the error r(t) obey a canonical second-order form:

r̈(t) + 2ξωnṙ(t) + ωn2r(t) = 0

With a damping ratio fixed at ξ = 0.8 to prevent oscillations, and a natural frequency ωn related to the response time Tr, the corrective gains

k1 = 2ξωn = 8⁄Tr    and    k2 = ωn2 = 16⁄ξ2 Tr2

guarantee smooth convergence and an optimal line of sight for the fixed observer.

3. Study No. 2: Circular Guidance for Target Observation

Combination of transit (alignment) and observation (circling) phases

When the drone enters the observation zone d < 100 m, the navigation law switches to circular guidance directly controlling the turn rate ψ from the deviation angle ξ relative to the line of sight:

ψ̇ = V2⁄Rref + K sin(η)⁄V

This theoretical formulation integrates a reference radius Rref and a corrective gain K (tested for K=0.2, K=1, and K=2) designed to ensure stable circling allowing 2 to 3 observation revolutions per target.

4. Critical Review & Improvement Avenues for the Simulink Model

Comparative analysis and identification of practical limitations of the specifications

Although navigation law switches operate correctly in Simulink, confronting mission requirements highlights clear technical limitations:

  • Lack of lap counting: The drone does not complete the 2 to 3 required revolutions around each target due to a lack of cumulative angle counting blocks integrated into the switching loop.
  • Gain sensitivity: Variations in gain K do not significantly affect the simulated trajectory, revealing a need for dynamic adjustment in the implementation of the heading controller.

// TECHNICAL ASSESSMENT & EVOLUTION PERSPECTIVES

This academic project helped deepen the design of control laws for non-linear aerospace systems under a MATLAB/Simulink environment, validating the compromise between rendezvous simplicity and geometric observation quality.

🎯 Key Skills & Contributions

  • ▸ Simulink Modeling: Design of multi-level block diagrams, multiports switching management, and integrated MATLAB functions.
  • ▸ Advanced Control: Application of potential guidance laws, second-order alignment ($\xi$, $\omega$), and circling.
  • ▸ Trajectory Analysis: Critical study of stability, response time, and multi-observer lines of sight.

🚀 Future Optimization Avenues

  • ▸ Counting Logic: Integration of a cumulative angle counter $\psi$ to guarantee the required number of revolutions prior to switching.
  • ▸ Realistic Dynamic Models: Replacement of the purely kinematic approach with a full dynamic drone model (force/moment vector).
  • ▸ Noise Robustness: Addition of disturbances and measurement noise on position sensors to test guidance law resilience.
