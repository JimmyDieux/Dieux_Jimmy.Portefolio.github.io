// PROJECT ARCHITECTURE: THE 3 KEY MODULES

To meet technical specifications and overcome environmental constraints of the test environment, the actuator control system is divided into three major complementary modules. Each fulfills a precise functional role in the testing and regulation chain.

1. The Thermal Module

Role: Physical barrier and management of conductive-convective fluxes

Principle: This is the external physical barrier enclosing the actuator. Its geometric design and material selection are aimed at absorbing extreme external thermal aggressions (ranging from -35°C in cold conditions up to +65°C in warm environments).

Utility: Without this protective barrier, the actuator would directly undergo thermal shocks from the external environment, immediately disrupting its mechanical properties, internal fluid viscosity, and stroke precision. This module creates a stable micro-climate around the component.

2. The Air Conditioning Module

Role: Supply of an external air flow to regulate the thermal module's temperature

Principle: This subsystem integrates an air conditioner located outside the test bench capable of generating an airflow at a constant temperature. It supplies the heat or refrigeration energy required to counter heat losses or gains through the casing.

Utility: The casing alone is not enough to block thermal transfers. It is imperative to inject a dynamic airflow to force the internal temperature to remain strictly within the safe target range (15°C to 30°C), thereby ensuring the actuator operates under nominal laboratory conditions regardless of the climatic conditions inside the test bench.

3. The Pneumatic & Regulation Module

Role: Control of actuator speed and force

Principle: This module manages the pneumatic supply and closed-loop control of the actuator at the heart of the test bench. It relies on the computer system governing the entire test bench.

Why it is present: It controls the dynamic response of the tested system. It is through this module that we control the actual speed of the actuator (calibrated between 0.01 and 0.1 m/s) and its capacity to develop a mechanical load force reaching up to 100 N.
