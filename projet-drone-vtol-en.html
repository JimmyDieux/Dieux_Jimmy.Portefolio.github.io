// PROJECT ARCHITECTURE: THE 3 IN-DEPTH DESIGN PILLARS

Developed collaboratively by the engineering team (Matthys Badts, Jimmy Dieux, Zachary Fery, Christopher Jumel, and Etienne Moyaux) as part of ELISA Aerospace's Drone Week, this project aims to meet critical medical transport requirements in highly tense environments.

1. Medical Use Case & Cargo Safety

Role: Rapid response for organ transplants, blood transfers, and biological serums

Time optimization: Designed to bypass road traffic congestion and complement traditional SAMU helicopter fleets, the LévyStation connects locations hundreds of kilometers apart while bypassing conventional runway constraints.

Logistics integration: The total internal volume of 140 liters is distributed between a front main hold and a rear hold, including a refrigerated compartment for temperature-sensitive samples.

Emergency systems: Integration of a ballistic emergency parachute deployment system ensuring the complete preservation of the payload in the event of a critical in-flight failure.

2. Hybrid Propulsion Architecture & Redundancy

Role: Thermal and electrical coupling for maximum endurance

Onboard generator: Use of a high-performance combustion engine operating as an electrical generator (technology similar to endurance motorsport standards), continuously powering the subsystems.

Multirotors and cruise propulsion: The system distributes power between 6 small vertical rotors (providing pure lift during vertical takeoff and landing phases with safety redundancy in case of engine failure) and two main cruise engines fixed on the empennage.

Energy balance: This hybrid compromise achieves a top speed of 300 km/h while maintaining a flight range exceeding 1,300 kilometers.

3. Numerical Modeling, CFD & SORA Regulation

Role: Finite element validation and EASA compliance

CAD design and flow: Structural studies conducted using SolidWorks and CFD numerical simulations under ANSYS FLUENT to validate the delicate aerodynamic transition between vertical (hover) flight and powered horizontal flight.

Regulatory framework: Rigorous integration of EASA and DGAC certification constraints (SORA risk analysis for integrating heavy civil drones into controlled airspace), in synergy with benchmark health stakeholders (EFS, Biomedicine Agency).
